Why we made it

Made for people who can't use a mouse

EyeSync is for people living with paralysis, or with a disability or injury that makes a mouse and keyboard hard or impossible to use. A webcam, face movement and voice can help them use a computer.

Read why we made EyeSync

What it does

A hands-free mouse, run from one tab

Steer with your head

The calibrated gaze model moves the real mouse across your screen. Hold a smile for a left click or open your mouth for a right click. Close both eyes for 1 second to start or stop offline voice typing, with a beep each time.

Every setting in reach

Pause, recenter, or change how you click right from the dashboard. Changes you make on the PC show up here instantly.

Tuned to you

A 30-second calibration learns how far you move, how you blink and how you smile. Calibrate each time you sit down for the best accuracy; an accuracy test shows how well the cursor lands.

Starts when you need it

Open the dashboard and EyeSync starts and connects by itself. Use the Quit control in the app or dashboard when you are finished.

Gestures

Your face is the mouse

Use a smile for left click, an open mouth for right click, and your voice for typing and on-screen keyboard commands.

  • Hold a smile

    Hold a closed-mouth smile for about 1.2 seconds.

    Left click

  • Open your mouth

    Open your mouth for about one second.

    Right click

  • Smile

    A big smile, held for 1.5 s.

    Pause or resume

  • Mouth to your left

    Pull your mouth to your left for 1.5 s. The cursor goes to the middle of the screen.

    Recenter

  • Mouth to your right

    Pull your mouth to your right for 3 s. A beep sounds after half a second, then a countdown shows on the camera view; relax your mouth to cancel.

    Calibrate

  • Tilt left

    Tilt your head to the left, ear towards your shoulder, and hold it. The further you tilt, the faster it scrolls. Straighten up to stop.

    Scroll up

  • Tilt right

    Tilt your head to the right and hold it. Straighten up to stop.

    Scroll down

  • Close both eyes

    Keep both eyes closed for 1 second, then talk. Close them again for 1 second to stop. A beep confirms each change.

    Voice typing

  • Mouth wide open

    Open your mouth wide for 3 s.

    Quit EyeSync

How it works

Up and running in three steps

  1. 1

    Download and install

    Download the EyeSync Standalone ZIP for Windows, extract it, and double-click Start EyeSync.bat. It does not install or update the original EyeSync.

    Download EyeSync
  2. 2

    Open your dashboard

    Create an account and open the Dashboard. EyeSync starts and connects by itself. No webcam handy? Try the built-in simulator first.

  3. 3

    Calibrate, every time

    Press Calibrate EyeSync and point your nose at the nine dots on your screen. EyeSync trains the pointer for your face, camera and display.

* You'll need Windows 10 or 11, a webcam, and Chrome, Edge or Firefox on the same computer.

Privacy

Private by design

  • The website runs in your browser and talks to EyeSync on this computer. The only online service it uses is Supabase, which keeps your account and settings.
  • The camera picture is processed on your PC. The optional camera view on the dashboard, and the live head demo on this page, run on your device and never upload or save a picture.
  • Your password is sent only to Supabase over HTTPS and kept as a bcrypt hash. The password itself is never saved, and no one else can read your account's data.
  • Cursor positions travel only from EyeSync to this page, and are never stored.
  • Voice typing runs on your PC, offline. What you say is typed there and never sent to this website.
  • For gaze jump, EyeSync works out where you look on your PC. Where you look is never sent to this website.
  • No cookies, no analytics, no third-party scripts. Even the fonts are bundled in.

Why we made it

Everyone deserves to use a computer.

We made EyeSync for people who can't use a mouse or keyboard: people living with paralysis, or with a disability or injury that makes moving their arms and hands hard or impossible. Using a computer shouldn't depend on your hands.

The problem

A computer is how we learn, work, talk to the people we love, find information and get everyday things done. Almost all of it is built around two hands on a mouse and a keyboard.

For someone who can't move their arms or hands, or can only move them a little, that can shut the door on all of it. Tools that help do exist, but special hardware can be expensive and hard to get.

About 1 in 6 people worldwide lives with a significant disability, according to the World Health Organization.

Our answer

EyeSync turns an ordinary webcam into a mouse. Its calibrated gaze model steers the cursor; a held smile left clicks and an open mouth right clicks. Its offline voice typing and command panel help with text and the on-screen keyboard.

It's free, it needs nothing but a webcam, and the camera picture never leaves your PC. We wanted something anyone could start using today, with what they already have.

Who it's for

Living with paralysis

People who can move their head but not their arms or hands, for example after a spinal cord injury.

Conditions that affect movement

People with conditions such as ALS (motor neurone disease), multiple sclerosis, muscular dystrophy or cerebral palsy, or after a stroke, for whom a mouse is hard or impossible to use.

Injury, pain and tremor

A broken arm, repetitive strain, arthritis or a tremor. For a few weeks, or for good.

Families and carers

Help set it up once, and the person you care for can use the computer on their own.

What we promise

  • Free. No price, no subscription, no ads.
  • No special hardware. The webcam you already have is enough.
  • Hands-free control. Move the cursor, click, and start voice typing with your face.
  • Private. Your camera picture, where you look and what you say stay on your PC.
  • Fitted to you. A 30-second calibration learns how you move, blink and smile.
  • Easy to reach. Big buttons, strong contrast and calm screens on this website, made for a head pointer.

Is EyeSync right for you?

EyeSync works best if you can move your head a little, even slightly, and blink or hold still to click. You'll need a Windows 10 or 11 computer with a webcam. Someone may need to help with the first install.

EyeSync is not a medical device. If it doesn't suit you, an occupational therapist or an assistive technology specialist can help you find what does.

We're Aniruddh and Rudra, and we're building EyeSync in the open. If it helps you, or something gets in your way, please tell us on GitHub. Every bit of feedback makes it better for the next person.
